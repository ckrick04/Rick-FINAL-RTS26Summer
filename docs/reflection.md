## FINAL REFLECTION
### Project
A safety critical system that simulates a ride's progression and emergency stops, built to demonstrate ride controls for a theme park engineering role.

### What I would do differently
If I were to take a similar course again, I would give myself more time to understand core concepts. Since I had an internship this summer, I broke my weekly schedule into distinct blocks where I would work on my internship on weekdays and work on the course on weekends. This gave me time to finish assignments, but it made it more difficult for concepts to sink in long-term. In future courses, I would review the concepts early by reading or skimming the learning materials at the beginning of the week, working on at least some of the exercises over the course of the week. This means that, by the weekend, I'm more prepared to complete the assignment rather than having to focus on learning concepts.

### What was harder than expected
The piece that I found the most difficult was understanding the ESP-IDF framework. The programming style used in the framework is different from other languages that I had used prior to the course. While I felt that I had a good understanding of the concepts, the framework felt unintuitive at times. In particular, trying to use the web monitor was difficult for me, and I relied heavily on the sample provided. There are several imports and functions required to get a basic web server to work, and it's difficult to trace through the individual functions and understand why they're necessary. This difficulty extended to concepts such as signaling and synchronization, where I had trouble with the "give and receive" architecture of semaphores in ESP-IDF. By reviewing examples and trying different modifications, I learned how an ISR can give up a semaphore and how the corresponding task can take it when it's ready. I believe that I could more effectively learn ESP-IDF and other microcontroller programming frameworks, but it would require more time spent on additional projects.

### The most valuable thing I learned
The most valuable thing I learned was how deadlines are set and the consequences of not meeting deadlines. Initially, I believed that setting deadlines was simple, and that it just involved understanding how long the user wants each task to take. Throughout the course, I learned that there were several other considerations. The worst case runtime (WCET) of the task has to be considered, and this is often estimated from a large sample of task runs. Along with the WCET, the utilization of the system matters, as high task WCETs with short deadlines can cause the system to become depleted of resources. I also found that understanding the goal of a real time system is very important, as this affects how tasks are classified and the consequences for missing deadlines. For critical systems, a single missed deadline can lead to a system-wide failure while for other systems, the result may simply be discarded or repurposed. From these lessons, I understand how to design a real-time system where all tasks reach their deadlines without failure.
